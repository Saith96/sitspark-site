How to stop a dog pulling on the leash

Dogs pull because it works: pulling gets them to the smell faster. Loose-leash walking flips that. A slack leash moves forward; a tight one goes nowhere.

1. Start indoors, with treats at your side

Reward your dog for standing next to your leg before you take a single step.

2. One step, reward by your leg

Then two steps, then five. Feed where you want your dog to be, not out in front.

3. Leash goes tight: stop

Stand still and stay quiet. The moment the leash slackens, mark it and walk on.

4. Let sniffing be the reward

Walk calmly to the lamp post, then say 'Go sniff'. The thing they pulled for becomes the prize for not pulling.

5. Keep a calm, slow pace

Rushing the walk winds your dog up. Short, calm practice walks beat one long tug of war.

By breed group. Northern breeds were bred to pull and need more patience and a well-fitted harness. Scent hounds follow their nose: build in plenty of sniff breaks.

Avoid choke, prong and shock collars. They suppress pulling through pain and can make dogs fearful or reactive.
